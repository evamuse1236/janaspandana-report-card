Presenter script: Janaspandana report card

About 5 minutes. Each "Click" = press → once. Say the line after it. Speak slowly; the screen does the rest.

Three things to understand before you present

1. What does "disposed" mean?

Three screens use three different words for the same thing:

Where Word it uses Number (22 Oct 2026)
Portal home page counter "Disposed" (ವಿಲೇವಾರಿ) about 480,000
Government dashboard (Power BI) "Resolved Grievances" 479,924
The citizen's app "Closed by Officer", then "Resolved (Feedback Taken)" your own complaints
  • On the dashboard, Total = Resolved + Pending, exactly (509,620 = 479,924 + 29,696). There is no third box. So every complaint that is no longer pending is called "resolved", including ones the officer closed that the citizen never confirmed.
  • In the app, the officer closing it ("Closed by Officer") and the citizen confirming it ("Resolved (Feedback Taken)") are two separate steps. The public numbers merge them.
  • Simple way to say it: "Disposed means the officer closed it. The dashboard even calls that 'resolved'. Neither means the citizen agreed it's fixed."
  • How sure we are: the words and the numbers are measured. That the dashboard's "Resolved" includes officer-closed complaints is arithmetic plus our reading (inferred): nothing else is left once pending is taken away.

2. How did we rate?

  • We picked 4 questions (indicators) a first-time village user would care about.
  • For each one we wrote a 1-to-5 scale with clear ends. Example for effort: 1 = more than 30 taps, 5 = fewer than 8 taps.
  • We put our evidence against that scale and chose the score as a group. These are our judgments, based on evidence, not official numbers.
  • Overall = the plain average: (2 + 2 + 3 + 3) ÷ 4 = 2.5 out of 5.

3. What data is it based on?

Source What we took from it
Our own test of the real app, Samsung S24 FE phone, 22 Oct 2026 Taps, steps, screens, what broke. We stopped before Submit, so we did not file a real complaint.
The official portal, ipgrs.karnataka.gov.in, read 22 Oct 2026 Dashboard numbers (509,571 filed, 479,243 disposed, 68.73% on time), who runs it, how escalation works.

Slide 1: Can I get my problem fixed? (2 clicks)

  • On open: "Every citizen with a complaint asks one question: can I get my problem fixed?"
  • Click 1: "We judged Karnataka's complaint app, Janaspandana, as a first-time user in a village with one shared phone. The government's Praja Seve Department runs it."
  • Click 2: "And we tested the real app, on a real phone, on 22 October. Watch this street light. It is off. Our whole talk is about getting it back on."

Slide 2: Five doors, one desk (3 clicks)

  • On open: "First: how do people complain?"
  • Click 1: "There are five ways in: the app, the website, the 1902 helpline, a service centre like Grama One, or on X. All of them land on one desk: Janaspandana."
  • Click 2: "Behind that desk sit more than 40 government departments. It started on 1 November 2021 and is open all the time."
  • Click 3: "Over five lakh complaints have been filed. Almost all are marked 'disposed'. But 'disposed' only means an officer closed it. It does not mean the problem is fixed. Keep that in mind."

Slide 3: Where a complaint goes (4 clicks)

  • On open: "This line shows a complaint's journey, using the app's own status names."
  • Click 1: "You file. You get a complaint number. An officer works on it, then closes it and uploads a report of what they did."
  • Click 2: "If you are not happy, you say so. The complaint goes up one level to a senior officer. Each level gets 7 days, 21 days in total. You can push it up only twice."
  • Click 3: "After the third level there is a wall. The app has no next step."
  • Click 4: "From here you must leave the app: ask for the records under RTI, complain about the officer to the Lokayukta, or use Sakala for late services."

Slide 4: 18 taps to report one street light (4 clicks)

  • On open: "We tried to report a dead street light, with a village address."
  • Click 1: "The form has five screens."
  • Click 2: "It took 18 taps and 5 list choices. It took us 140 seconds, and we knew the way. A new user would need about 3 to 6 minutes; that is our guess."
  • Click 3: "The search box found nothing. Some lists never loaded, even after 30 seconds."
  • Click 4: "There is no button to speak instead of type. And after filing, no screen we saw showed a due date."

Slide 5: We asked four questions (4 clicks)

  • On open: "We judged the app on four questions, each scored from 1 to 5."
  • Click 1: "One: digital literacy. Can someone with little phone skill finish on their own?"
  • Click 2: "Two: accountability after closure. If it is closed but not fixed, can I reopen it and see who answers?"
  • Click 3: "Three: effort. How many taps, minutes and choices does it take?"
  • Click 4: "Four: trust. Can I see the status, the deadline and honest results? Two of these, effort and after closure, measure what a real user goes through, not what the website promises."

Slide 6: The report card (5 clicks)

  • On open: "Here is our report card. Each lit lamp is one point out of five."
  • Click 1: "Digital literacy: 2. Kannada is one tap away, which is good. But you climb five lists of officials' words, and you can't speak your problem."
  • Click 2: "Accountability after closure: 2. The rules exist on paper, 21 days and 3 levels. But users write that they could not reopen, and one found no feedback button."
  • Click 3: "Effort: 3. 18 taps is not terrible, but the search is silent and some lists are dead."
  • Click 4: "Trust: 3. The dashboard is public, which is good. It also shows that 3 in 10 complaints miss the deadline."
  • Click 5: "Overall: 2.5 out of 5. Easy to open. Hard to finish. Weak after closure."

Slide 7: Same app, three journeys (4 clicks)

  • On open: "The same app feels different in a city, a town and a village."
  • Click 1: "In the city there is one extra step: you must pick your ward officer."
  • Click 2: "Towns have service centres nearby to help."
  • Click 3: "In a village, you depend on a helper, a Grama One centre, or the 1902 phone line. Your own phone, data and typing are the limits; that is our guess."
  • Click 4: "Some people are left out entirely: people with no phone number, people who can't type, problems that involve several departments, and topics the app refuses, like court cases."

Slide 8: Closed is not fixed (4 clicks)

  • On open: "These are real screens from the app, showing 'Closed by Officer'."
  • Click 1: "This is what the app says: closed."
  • Click 2: "This is what the citizen says: 'Resolved by the officer, but the street is still not having street light.' The poles are still lying on the ground."
  • Click 3: "Another person: 'Marked solved without any real action.' Their land access was blocked for years."
  • Click 4: "In February 2024, over 20,000 people went to the Vidhana Soudha to meet the Chief Minister in person. He said if officers solved problems locally, people would not need to come to Bengaluru. These reviews are not a survey, so we use them only as warning signs."

Slide 9: Seven fixes, where they land (4 clicks)

  • On open: "Here is the same journey again, with our seven fixes pinned where they help."
  • Click 1: "Most fixes belong at the very first step: make search work, cut the lists, let people speak or send a photo, and use plain words."
  • Click 2: "While it is in progress: show the days left and the officer's name."
  • Click 3: "At closure: always allow reopening, with the report as proof. And when a list fails to load, show an error and the helpline number."
  • Click 4: "Do this, and 'resolved' will finally mean the light is on. Thank you."

Slide 10: Questions we expect (no clicks)

Keep this slide for questions. Short answers: - "What is the official time limit?" "21 days, 7 per level, since May 2025. The '15 days' you may have heard was the CM's spoken order for one meeting in 2023." - "Does disposed mean fixed?" "No. Disposed means the officer closed it. The public numbers don't show how many citizens agreed it was fixed." - "Why give trust 3 when you couldn't see the status screens?" "The dashboard and the levels are public. The screens after filing we could not test, and we say so." - "Did you file a real complaint?" "No. We stopped just before Submit." - "What if all three levels fail?" "RTI for the records, the Lokayukta for officer misconduct, or Sakala for late services. We could not confirm Sakala's current compensation rates."

If someone asks something you don't know

Say: "We could not confirm that; it's in our list of open questions." The open list is in the FAQ: rating scale, deadline to reopen, transfers between departments, and who exactly the level 2 and 3 officers are.
